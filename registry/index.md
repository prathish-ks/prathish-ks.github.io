Dated, falsifiable forecasts about enterprise software markets. Each carries a confidence level,
a leading indicator you can check yourself, and a resolution date.

**Resolved forecasts stay here whether they landed or not.** Publishing the misses is the point —
a track record you can only see when it flatters me is not a track record.

[The commit history of this site](https://github.com/prathish-ks/prathish-ks.github.io/commits/main)
is the dated record — check it yourself. Entries are not edited after publication; corrections
appear as new rows.

| 1 | [2026-10-17](/2026/10/commonwealth-vmware-contracts/) | Of the 19 Commonwealth VMware contracts identified in that analysis, more than half by count will be followed, within 12 months of expiry, by a new AusTender notice from the same agency naming VMware or its successor entity as supplier | 65% | Nutanix + Proxmox as a share of advertised VMware roles in Australia. 11% at baseline; a durable rise above 15% weakens the call | 2030-02-28 |
| 2 | [2026-11-02](/2026/11/learn-the-migration/) | Advertised Australian roles naming Nutanix or Proxmox, as a share of advertised roles naming VMware, will exceed 15% — up from 9.6% at the baseline measurement | 40% | Public procurement notices for alternative virtualisation platforms, particularly from agencies that already hold VMware contracts. That indicates migration investment; it does not establish that the work will be hired for in Australia | 2028-07-29 |

**Forecasts 1 and 2 test different things and can both land.** The first is about nineteen named
Commonwealth contracts and whether they are followed by another VMware notice. The second is
about alternative-platform hiring across Australia, most of which has nothing to do with those
nineteen. Both succeed if those agencies renew while building migration capability, or if
adoption grows elsewhere. What a sustained rise in alternative hiring would weaken is the
*reasoning* behind forecast 1 — that an exit is hard to staff — rather than its outcome.

Forecast 2 measures one job board's advertised counts, which is an indirect signal of migration
rather than a measure of it. Should the board materially change its search coverage, query
handling or how it counts results, the change will be documented here and the forecast may be
recorded inconclusive rather than settled on a measurement that is no longer comparable.
